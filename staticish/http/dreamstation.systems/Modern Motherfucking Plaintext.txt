§ Y̲o̲u̲r̲ ̲q̲u̲o̲t̲e̲s̲ ̲a̲r̲e̲ ̲f̲u̲c̲k̲i̲n̲g̲ ̲d̲u̲m̲b̲

"These" are “dumb quotes”. They are called that because they make you look 
dumb. They are not a real thing — you will never see a book that 
"prints dialogue like this". They were a convenience for typewriters that were 
too cheap to add another button, they were carried forward into ASCII 
because they were too cheap to add more bits, and they remain today as an ASCII 
compatibility character.

“These” are actual fucking English quotation marks. They open and then they 
close.

By the way, the apostrophe is not ' either. It’s ’.

’, not ‘, is also used for apostrophes of omission (’90s, not ‘90s). Yes, your 
typesetting software gets this wrong.

§ -̲ ̲i̲s̲ ̲n̲o̲t̲ ̲‐̲ ̲i̲s̲ ̲n̲o̲t̲ ̲‑̲ ̲i̲s̲ ̲n̲o̲t̲ ̲–̲ ̲i̲s̲ ̲n̲o̲t̲ ̲—̲ ̲i̲s̲ ̲n̲o̲t̲ ̲−̲

The character on the - button on your keyboard is called a HYPHEN-MINUS¹. It, 
too, is not a real character. It is called a HYPHEN-MINUS because it was a 
typewriter convenience designed to serve as all of a minus sign or a hyphen 
or “anything that happens to look like that”. There are more specific 
characters for each job. Use them:

 • ‐, the hyphen, for compounds
 • ‑, the non‐breaking hyphen, for keeping things like names on one line
 • –, the en dash, for ranges and relations (9–5; New York–London flights)
 • —, the em dash, for a break in thought — like this one
 • −, the actual minus sign

¹Yes, I know. Unicode officially gives all characters a name in ASCII, and 
I’m using that name.

It’s café, not cafe. Łódź, São Paulo, Nguyễn, Zoë, Beyoncé, Björk. Spell
